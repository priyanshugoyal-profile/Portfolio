Campaign UTM & Taxonomy Governance Engine

Finds every broken tracking link before it poisons your attribution — then fixes them, collapses the duplicates, and hands your team a naming standard they can't break.

The problem

Nobody governs UTM parameters. One person writes Facebook, another writes fb, a third leaves a space in the campaign name. Analytics tools treat every variant as a separate campaign, so one real campaign shows up as four rows with the spend split between them. Every month someone spends hours reconciling it in a spreadsheet, and every optimisation decision before that cleanup was made on broken numbers.

3. Phantom campaigns

Campaign names are normalised (case, separators, whitespace stripped) and then compared with Levenshtein edit distance to catch typos. Each cluster below is one real campaign that your analytics tool is currently reporting as several.

4. Governed link builder

The fix isn't cleaning links once — it's making bad links impossible to create. This builder only emits URLs that satisfy the registry and the naming convention above.

How it works

The audit is pure deterministic logic — it runs identically every time, with or without an API key. That matters: governance rules you can't reproduce aren't governance.

1
Parse

Each row is parsed with the native URL API. Malformed links are caught and reported rather than silently dropped.

2
Validate against registry

Source and medium are checked against approved lists, plus a synonym map that resolves ppc, paid_search and cpc to one canonical value.

3
Cluster

Campaign names are normalised and compared with Levenshtein distance scaled to name length, so typos merge but genuinely different campaigns stay apart.

4
Quantify

Spend attached to non-canonical variants is summed — turning a hygiene problem into a number a CMO will act on.

5
Auto-fix

Corrected URLs are regenerated from canonical values. Anything the engine can't fix without guessing is flagged for a human instead of invented.

6
Prevent

The builder and the exported standard close the loop, so the same mess doesn't reappear next quarter.

Everything above works with no key. Adding one lets Claude write the plain-English rationale section of the exported taxonomy standard — the part that gets stakeholders to actually follow it. Your key is held in sessionStorage, sent only to api.anthropic.com, and dropped when the tab closes.
