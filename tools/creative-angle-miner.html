Marketing Ops Automation

Creative Performance Post-Mortem

Ad platforms tell you which creative won. They never tell you which angle won — so teams relearn the same lesson every quarter. This tags every creative and reports by angle, with an explicit line between signal and noise.

The problem

After a campaign, someone exports the creative report and spends a day in a spreadsheet hand-labelling each ad — was it the discount hook or the social proof? — trying to work out what to make next. It's slow, it's inconsistent between analysts, and it usually ends with a confident conclusion drawn from four conversions. The expensive mistake isn't the lost day. It's scaling a "winner" that was noise.

How it works

The arithmetic and the confidence gating are deterministic. The model, if you enable it, only improves the tagging step — and its tags go through exactly the same statistics afterwards.

1
Parse & compute

CSV in, then CTR, CVR, CPA and ROAS per creative. Rows missing required columns are reported, not silently dropped.

2
Classify

A word-boundary keyword classifier scores each headline across angles and hook types, and the highest score wins. The full keyword basis is visible in the page.

3
Aggregate by tag

Spend, conversions and revenue are pooled per angle and per hook — turning 38 noisy creative rows into 6 decisions.

4
Gate on confidence

Each group gets a 95% interval from its conversion count. Groups under the volume floor, or whose interval straddles the blended average, are labelled as such and excluded from recommendations.

5
Simulate

Budget moves are modelled at the winner's observed CPA, with the assumption stated on screen rather than buried.

6
Recommend

Untested combinations are ranked by the product of their angle and hook multipliers; creatives losing money at scale are listed for cutting.

The keyword classifier handles clear cases well and struggles with irony, implication and mixed messages. With a key, Claude re-tags every headline instead — and the result is fed through the same deterministic statistics, so the model never gets to decide what is significant. Your key stays in sessionStorage, goes only to api.anthropic.com, and is dropped when the tab closes.
