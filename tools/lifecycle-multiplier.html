A story about an afternoon lost

One message, four places, nothing broken

You have written the campaign. Now it has to become an email, a text, a WhatsApp message and a notification on someone's lock screen — each with its own fiddly rules that nobody keeps in their head.

The copy-and-paste afternoon

So you write them all out. You guess at the lengths, because who actually knows how long a push notification can be. You upload them. Half come back rejected. You fix those, upload again, and something else trips.

The nastiest one never announces itself at all. Write your text message in Word, or paste it from an email, and you will pick up a curly apostrophe — ’ instead of '. It looks identical. It is not. That one character can double what the whole send costs you, and nothing anywhere will warn you. You just get a bigger invoice at the end of the month and never find out why.

None of this is hard. It is just tedious, easy to get wrong, and completely invisible until it costs money.

Step 3 · The rules it is checking

Here they all are, in the open, so you can disagree with any of them. A rule you cannot see is just someone else's guess with extra confidence.

WhereWhich bitLimitWhy it matters
The apostrophe that doubles your bill. Phones have two ways of storing a text message. The cheap way handles the ordinary characters on your keyboard and fits 160 of them into one message. The expensive way handles everything else — curly quotes, long dashes, emoji — and only fits 70.

Here is the catch: one unusual character anywhere in the message switches the whole thing to the expensive way. So a perfectly normal 110-character text suddenly needs two messages instead of one, and your send costs twice as much. Nothing warns you, because to a human the two apostrophes look identical. The counter above spots it and tells you exactly which character did it.

Step 4 · When to send what

Cheapest and least annoying first, saving the intrusive ones for people who did not respond. The warnings underneath look at the whole run of messages together, not one at a time — because “is this one message fine?” is the wrong question when someone is getting four of them.

Step 5 · Take it away

Ready to paste into whatever you send with. Anything still broken is held back by default, which is rather the point — nothing that would be rejected should ever reach the upload screen.

What this tool actually does

The writing is the easy half. The checking is the half that saves you the afternoon.

1
Takes your brief once

What you are selling, what puts people off, what you want them to do. Every version below is built from those same few answers, so the message stays consistent.

2
Writes each version

Three different approaches per place — one leads with the benefit, one answers the objection, one uses the deadline — rather than the same sentence reworded three times.

3
Counts properly

Not just characters. It works out how your phone network will actually split and bill the message, including the expensive-alphabet trap.

4
Checks as you type

Lengths, where text gets cut off on a real phone, and WhatsApp's fussy rules about where you can put a name. Every keystroke, instantly.

5
Looks at the whole run

Four messages in a week is a lot. Someone on the other side of the world gets your 9am send at 2am. It says so.

6
Holds back what is broken

Anything that would be rejected is left out of the download unless you deliberately tick the box.

Why the boring half is the valuable half. Plenty of things will write you four versions of a message. Almost nothing checks them against the rules of the place they are going. So the copy arrives looking finished, gets rejected at upload, and someone spends the afternoon counting characters by hand to find out why. The writing was never the bottleneck.
